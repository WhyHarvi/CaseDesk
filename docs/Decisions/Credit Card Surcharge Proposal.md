# Credit Card Surcharge Proposal

## Context

QuickBooks Payments charges a processing fee (currently deducted silently
from what the agency's merchant deposit shows) on every card and bank
transfer transaction made through its hosted invoice payment page. The
agency absorbs both today. Goal, as decided: pass **both** costs to the
client — 2.4% on credit card, 1% on bank transfer — without touching the
existing manual e-transfer/cash/cheque/wire/debit flows, which aren't
processed through QuickBooks' hosted page at all and carry no such fee.

There is already a per-agency configurable rate for a *related* purpose:
`AgencyQuickBooksSettings.refundFeeRatePercent` (`prisma/schema.prisma:1380`,
default 2.99%), used only to estimate how much a **refund** shrinks after
Intuit's fee (`backend/src/services/refundFeeEstimateService.js`) — never
applied to an outgoing charge. The rate itself is admin-editable per agency
today (Settings → Payments & Fees), which is the right existing precedent:
whatever surcharge rate gets built should be similarly per-agency and
editable, not a hardcoded 3.25%, since Intuit's actual rate varies by
merchant/plan and can change.

## How card payments actually work today (why this isn't a simple line-item add)

Traced through [[QuickBooks Online]] and [[Billing and Payments]]:

1. CaseDesk creates a normal accounting-API invoice
   (`createQuickBooksInvoice`, `backend/src/services/quickbooksService.js:480-556`)
   with **both** `AllowOnlineCreditCardPayment: true` and
   `AllowOnlineACHPayment: true` hardcoded on every invoice.
2. It immediately calls QuickBooks' own `/invoice/{id}/send` to a
   non-deliverable address purely to force Intuit to provision the hosted
   "Pay now" `InvoiceLink` (lines 541-553) — nothing is actually emailed by
   QuickBooks; CaseDesk delivers that link itself.
3. The client opens the link and picks bank transfer (ACH/pre-authorized
   debit, QuickBooks' own hosted-page option — **distinct from the
   existing manual Interac e-transfer flow** in [[QuickBooks Online]]'s
   Payments settings, which never touches this link at all) **or** card,
   entirely inside Intuit's own hosted checkout page. CaseDesk has no
   visibility into that choice until the payment lands (webhook or poll).
4. CaseDesk's QuickBooks OAuth connection only requests
   `com.intuit.quickbooks.accounting` scope
   (`quickbooksService.js:87`) — no `com.intuit.quickbooks.payments`
   scope, no charge/token endpoints. CaseDesk cannot process a card charge
   itself or inject conditional pricing into Intuit's checkout.
5. For payment-schedule installments specifically, the invoice (and its
   `InvoiceLink`) is generated by `fireInstallment`
   (`backend/src/services/paymentScheduleService.js:714-753`) reading the
   installment's `amount` **verbatim** — well before the client ever picks
   a payment method. A surcharge cannot be baked into the installment
   amount at schedule-creation time; the payment method isn't known yet.

Net: CaseDesk cannot charge "the fee, only if they pay by card" as a single
invoice the way a checkout page with two buttons could. The fee either has
to be an amount decided *before* the client reaches Intuit's page (so the
client must declare their method first), or handled as a follow-up charge
*after* Intuit tells CaseDesk which method was actually used.

## Options

### Option A — Ask Intuit first (no code) — checked, ruled out
**Confirmed with QuickBooks (Simple Start plan): no native automatic
surcharge/convenience-fee feature exists.** The only supported mechanism is
manually adding the surcharge as an ordinary invoice line item — a Service
product (e.g. "Credit card surcharge") mapped to an income account, with
the dollar amount computed and entered by hand (QuickBooks does not
auto-calculate a percentage-based line). This is the same "just an invoice
line" mechanism already described under Data model impact below — Intuit
isn't doing anything CaseDesk couldn't also do, it's simply confirming
there's no magic conditional-on-card-payment toggle to lean on instead.
Net effect: **the timing problem doesn't go away** — a line item, whether
added by a human in QuickBooks or by CaseDesk automatically, still has to
be decided before the client reaches the hosted checkout, because nothing
on Intuit's side changes the invoice based on which method the client
picks there. This makes Option B effectively mandatory, not just preferred.

One useful, unprompted piece of guidance from that research: map the
surcharge item to **Other Income**, not the agency's regular service-fee
income account — keeping it separate from professional-service revenue.
This lines up with (and validates) the `countsTowardRevenue: false` call
already made below for the `AgencyFeeCategory` row.

### Option B — Client declares intent, CaseDesk sends the matching invoice
Since both online methods now carry their own disclosed fee (decided: 2.4%
card, 1% bank transfer — no fee-free online option remains; only the
existing manual e-transfer/cash/cheque/wire/debit paths stay fee-free),
this is really "generate the invoice for whichever total matches the
method the client already committed to," each with only its own method
enabled: add a payment-method choice before the invoice is generated —
"Pay by bank transfer — $X × 1.01" vs. "Pay by card — $X × 1.024" — then
create the matching QuickBooks invoice with the *other* method turned off
via `AllowOnlineACHPayment` / `AllowOnlineCreditCardPayment` (confirmed:
these flags are designed to restrict which methods the hosted page offers,
per Intuit's own intent for the field — see Compliance below). So the
bank-transfer invoice gets `AllowOnlineCreditCardPayment: false`, and the
card invoice gets `AllowOnlineACHPayment: false` — each invoice enforces
its own total against its own single allowed method.

Trade-off: adds a step to checkout (client must commit to a method before
seeing the invoice). The commitment is not a hard wall: until any funds are
received, the client can reopen the choice and CaseDesk updates the same
QuickBooks invoice in place. The original base amount is used each time so a
new processing fee replaces, rather than compounds, the prior fee. Payment or
refund activity locks the choice to preserve accounting history.

### Option C — Charge the surcharge after the fact
Let the existing single invoice/link stand as-is (both methods allowed, no
proactive surcharge). Once a payment lands, read the payment method back
via `getQuickBooksPayment` (`quickbooksService.js:636-649`, which already
reads `PaymentMethodRef` / `CreditCardPayment.CreditChargeResponse` off the
Intuit-created Payment object) or the `Payment` webhook event
(`quickbooksWebhookService.js:129-137`). If it was a card payment, generate
a small follow-up invoice for `amount × rate`.

Trade-off: two client-facing charges instead of one; the client didn't see
the surcharge before agreeing to pay, which is worse for disclosure/consent
than A or B and may not satisfy card-network surcharge-disclosure rules
(see Compliance below) — probably the weakest option on that basis alone,
even though it's the least invasive technically.

### Recommendation
Option A is ruled out (see above). Option B — the client-declares-intent
flow, each method's invoice enforcing its own fee — is the decided path:
it's the only one that discloses each fee to the client *before* they pay
(which Compliance below treats as close to mandatory), and the only one
that prevents a client from paying one method's invoice through the
other, now that both methods carry a real fee to get right.

### Implemented fallback and offline branch

The client portal also offers the existing fee-free manual methods without
sending the client through the hosted QuickBooks payment page. Interac
e-Transfer, debit, and other arrangements create the base QuickBooks invoice
with both hosted methods disabled; a submitted reference or screenshot is
unverified evidence only and cannot change the invoice balance. Staff verifies
receipt and records the payment through the existing audited manual-payment
workflow.

The portal always displays the configured surcharge rates before selection. If
a dedicated QuickBooks item is missing, CaseDesk reuses an exact active item or
creates the system-owned Service item under an active `Other Income` account,
then persists both mapping records before creating or updating the invoice. A
workspace with no eligible income account receives a specific setup error;
CaseDesk never silently changes a configured fee to zero.

## Data model impact (regardless of option chosen)

- **Fee rates**: two new fields —
  `AgencyQuickBooksSettings.cardSurchargeRatePercent` (2.4%) and
  `bankTransferFeeRatePercent` (1%) — both kept **separate** from
  `refundFeeRatePercent` (decided — these serve different purposes and can
  legitimately diverge from each other and from the refund-estimate rate).
- **Client province**: `Client.address` (`prisma/schema.prisma:2458`) is a
  single free-text field today — no structured province column, so a
  Quebec exclusion can't be built against existing data. Decided: add a
  structured province field to the Client model so the exclusion can be
  automatic rather than manual/policy-based. This is a real schema change
  and its own migration, not just a surcharge-feature detail — scope it
  accordingly when this gets built (and decide whether it's a full
  address-structuring pass or just enough for this).
- **Line items**: `CaseInvoiceLine` (`schema.prisma:940-960`) already has a
  generic shape (`feeCategory`, `unitAmount`, `taxable`, `taxRate`,
  `lineTotal`) that both fee lines can reuse via two new
  `AgencyFeeCategory` (`schema.prisma:1405-1425`) rows — e.g.
  `kind: "CardSurcharge"` and `kind: "BankTransferFee"`. No schema change
  strictly required for the lines themselves.
- **Incentive/revenue exclusion**: `AgencyFeeCategory.countsTowardRevenue`
  already exists and defaults `false`; [[Billing and Payments]] confirms
  disbursement/government/college categories are already excluded from
  revenue-contest credit for the same reason a pass-through fee should be —
  it isn't agency income. Set it `false` on both fee categories so
  [[Incentive Expansion Requirements]] crediting isn't affected.
- **Tax treatment**: per the CRA's 2023 technical interpretation (see
  Compliance below), a compliant **card** surcharge is HST-exempt — so
  `taxable` on the card-surcharge line should default `false`, contingent
  on meeting the conditions in Compliance below. The **bank-transfer fee's**
  tax treatment is a separate, unresearched question (see Compliance) —
  don't default it to the same `taxable: false` without checking, since
  the CRA reasoning was specific to card-network surcharge rules. Confirm
  both with the agency's accountant before relying on either — CRA
  technical interpretations aren't binding legislation.
- **Refunds — decided: surcharge is not refunded.** When a surcharged
  invoice is refunded, the client gets the base amount back, not the
  surcharge — same reasoning already encoded in
  `refundFeeEstimateService.js` (QuickBooks charges a *new* processing fee
  on the refund transaction itself, so the original fee was never
  recoverable by the agency either). Which underlying fee categories are
  refundable at all (e.g. professional/service fees vs. government or
  consultation fees, which the agency says follow their own separate
  rules) is an existing policy this proposal doesn't change — it only
  needs to make sure the surcharge line itself is tagged/excluded
  consistently with whatever that existing refund-eligibility logic
  already does for non-refundable fee categories.

## Compliance (researched — 3.25% does not survive this)

Agency is in Ontario. Researched directly rather than trusting the 3.25%
figure given; findings below are sourced (see Sources at the bottom) and
should still be confirmed with a lawyer/accountant before shipping, but
they change the shape of this proposal, not just a detail:

- **3.25% is very likely not a legal rate to charge.** Since the October 6,
  2022 Visa/Mastercard class-action settlement, Canadian merchants may
  surcharge credit cards, but the surcharge is capped at **the lesser of
  2.4% or the merchant's actual cost of acceptance** — never a number the
  merchant simply picks. 2.4% is a hard ceiling from the card networks
  themselves, not a suggestion.
- **QuickBooks Payments' actual Canadian online-invoice card rate is 2.9% +
  $0.25/transaction** — meaning even surcharging at the legal maximum
  (2.4%) does not fully recover the real cost; a gap remains (the flat
  $0.25 plus the ~0.5% above the cap) that the agency keeps absorbing
  regardless of what's built. "Client pays the full fee" isn't fully
  achievable within legal bounds — only "client pays up to 2.4%,
  agency absorbs the rest."
- **Bank transfer also gets a disclosed fee — decided: 1%,** matching
  QuickBooks' own real cost for that method (unlike the card surcharge,
  1% recovers the actual cost exactly; there's no shortfall to absorb on
  this side). **Important, unresearched distinction: the 2.4% cap, the
  Visa/Mastercard rules, and the CRA's HST-exemption technical
  interpretation above are all specifically about credit card
  surcharging** — they come from card-network rules that don't govern
  bank transfer/ACH at all, since no card network is involved. Whether a
  1% "bank transfer processing fee" needs different disclosure treatment,
  has its own tax characterization (it may not qualify for the same
  financial-service HST exemption reasoning, which was argued specifically
  from card-network surcharge rules), or is fine to treat identically to
  the card fee, hasn't been checked — don't assume it inherits the card
  surcharge's compliance story just because the mechanism (an invoice line
  item) looks the same.
- **Ontario permits surcharging; Quebec bans it outright** under Quebec's
  Consumer Protection Act. If any clients are billed from a Quebec
  mailing/business address, the surcharge must not apply to them — see
  the structured province field decided under Data model impact above.
- **Debit must never be surcharged** — the cap and the entire surcharge
  mechanism applies to credit cards only. `MANUAL_PAYMENT_METHODS` already
  lists `"Debit"` as a distinct method from anything card-related
  (`caseInvoiceService.js:27`), so the existing model already separates
  them; just don't let a surcharge category get attached to a debit-method
  payment.
- **Disclosure**: the rate must be posted before the client completes
  payment — practically, this means a visible notice on whatever payment
  surface CaseDesk builds under Option B, not just an invoice line that
  appears after the fact. A silent line item on the emailed invoice alone
  is likely not sufficient disclosure on its own.
- **Advance notice — resolved: no separate filing needed.** For a
  QuickBooks Payments merchant account specifically, there isn't a manual
  "notify the acquirer" step the agency files itself — surcharging
  eligibility/compliance is handled at the payments-program level (what
  QuickBooks Payments supports for the account/region), not something
  submitted ad hoc. What still matters, and isn't optional, is the
  customer-facing disclosure point above — that's the actual compliance
  surface here, not a network filing.
- **GST/HST (Ontario's HST)**: the CRA's March 2023 technical
  interpretation treats a compliant card surcharge as an **exempt
  financial service** (not subject to HST) — but only if all of: it's
  charged solely for accepting the card and not charged for other payment
  methods; it's charged by the agency itself, not a payment processor
  acting as billing agent; it complies with the card network's surcharge
  rules (the 2.4% cap above); and it's shown/charged as its own separate
  line. Charging above 2.4% likely forfeits this exemption on top of
  breaking the network rules — another reason the rate matters, not just
  the disclosure.

Bottom line: replace 3.25% with **2.4%, or the agency's actual cost of
acceptance if lower** — and get that confirmed by the agency's
accountant/lawyer against current rules before this goes live, since I'm
not the authority on either.

## Effort estimate

Medium for Option B: two new agency settings (card rate capped at 2.4% in
the UI itself, not just documented, plus the bank-transfer rate), two new
fee categories, a payment-method-choice step in whatever surfaces send the
invoice link (case billing workspace, payment-schedule installment firing,
client portal), the `AllowOnlineACHPayment`/`AllowOnlineCreditCardPayment`
per-invoice branching in `quickbooksService.js` wired per-invoice instead
of hardcoded (now used both ways, not just to disable ACH), a structured
`Client` province field plus its own migration for the Quebec exclusion,
and disclosure copy on whatever payment surface is built. Each piece is
individually small and the two fees share one mechanism, so this doesn't
double the work — but the compliance/tax questions (especially the
bank-transfer fee's still-unresearched treatment) need answers before "how
it's coded" is the remaining question.
